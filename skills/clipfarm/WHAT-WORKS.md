# What makes a GTA clip go viral

Research behind the hook and timing rules in `SKILL.md`. Collected 2026-11-13 from YouTube Shorts: the top Shorts
on 12 GTA hashtag pages (2,355 Shorts), full stats on the 211 most viewed, and 12 of the real gameplay ones watched
second by second with their top comments.

## What the most viewed GTA Shorts actually are
Of the 211 most viewed (7 M to 264 M views each), only 21 are real gameplay moments: something a player captured
and posted. The rest are kids' mod "challenges" (Spider-Man vs a footballer and so on), fake "GTA 6" skits and AI
videos, staged troll mods and live-action memes.

The real moments get the real engagement. Since March 2025 YouTube counts a Shorts view on any play, so likes and
comments per view are the honest signal:

| | Shorts | Median views | Likes per view | Comments per 10k views | Median length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real gameplay moments | 21 | 13.3 M | **1.6 %** | **0.94** | 23 s |
| Kids' mod challenges | 65 | 14.4 M | 0.46 % | 0.02 | 23 s |
| Fake "GTA 6" / AI / skits | 71 | 17.2 M | 0.73 % | 0.06 | 36 s |
| Staged troll mods | 11 | 9.9 M | 0.55 % | 0.05 | 52 s |

"GTA 6" in a title pulls views before launch, which is why the fake ones are everywhere. Never label GTA V footage
as GTA 6.

## What the real ones have in common (12 watched end to end)
1. **The caption is a joke setup and the clip is the punchline.** Every one has a single burned-in caption at the
   top, in the bar above a centred clip. It frames the chaos as something ordinary:
   - a quote, then who answers it (someone says they'll be late, then `Dad:` and a car flying across the city),
     the most common format;
   - `POV:` plus a moment;
   - a label for what we're watching (how a parent imagines you drive their car);
   - confidence the clip is about to break (he'll surely be fine; he thought he'd lost me).
   About half end in one emoji (💀 😭 😂).
2. **The comments continue the joke.** The top comments either quote the payoff or post their own version of the
   caption (a dad at the airport hours early, a taxi driver who knows a shortcut). A caption people can riff on is
   what turns views into comments.
3. **No intro.** Action and sound start on frame 0, with the caption already there.
4. **The payoff sits in the last third** in most, and the clip cuts within about a second of it (or holds a beat
   on the wreck). Stunt clips are the exception: the launch starts at once and the landing comes mid-clip. Every
   one loops straight back to the start; none has an end card. They run 15–40 s because their action is long (a
   chase, a long flight); a single crash doesn't need that.
5. **A centred clip with bars or a blurred copy behind it**, not a crop to full screen, so the whole action stays
   visible and the caption has room. (Clipfarm's `"frame"` layout.)
6. **Game sound carries it.** Half add a music track or meme sounds; in the rest the crash or the scream is the
   payoff.
7. **Titles are a second short joke with one emoji**, a few words long: early, a shortcut, they didn't see it
   coming.
